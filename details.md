## 10. Visual and UX direction

- **Overall feel:** Clean, reassuring, professional, accessible, and healthcare-oriented.
- **Suggested palette:** White/off-white backgrounds, calm medical blue or teal accents, dark navy text, and restrained green accents. This is a design suggestion, not an existing brand palette supplied by the business.
- **Typography:** Highly legible sans-serif; support both English and Devanagari/Hindi.
- **Photography:** Use genuine photos of the actual lab, reception, sample collection setup, staff (with consent), and signage. Do not use stock photos in a way that falsely depicts the facility or staff.
- **Mobile-first:** Make the phone CTA, home collection CTA, address, and directions easy to find.
- **Accessibility:** Strong contrast, readable type, descriptive labels, keyboard-accessible forms, and clear error messages.
- **Forms:** Collect only necessary contact details. Do not ask visitors to submit sensitive medical reports or health information through an unsecured form.

## 11. Medical, privacy, and trust requirements

- Do not promise “100% accuracy,” guaranteed diagnosis, or guaranteed report delivery times.
- A laboratory report is not a medical diagnosis; encourage patients to consult a qualified healthcare professional for interpretation.
- Do not publish claims such as NABL/ISO accreditation, government approval, specialist qualifications, or advanced equipment unless documentary proof is supplied and verified.
- Do not publish patient reports, names, phone numbers, or other personal/medical information without explicit consent and appropriate safeguards.
- Any online enquiry form should clearly explain how submitted information will be used and protected.
- Avoid claiming that all tests are available until the current test menu is confirmed.
